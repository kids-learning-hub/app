Hearing the first sound of a word is the skill teachers call phonemic awareness, and it is one of the best predictors of how easily a child will learn to read. These sheets train it with pictures: the child says the word, listens to its first sound and finds the matching letter. The first level offers three letters to choose from, the second asks the child to write it, and the third moves to the last letter, which is harder to hear.

🅰️ Best for ages 4–7

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Say the word with your child, exaggerating the first sound: “mmm-moon”.
  • Say the sound of the letter (“mmm”), not only its name (“em”).
  • If a picture has two possible names, accept the one your child uses and check the sound.
  • Make it a game at home: “I spy something that starts with…”.

Frequently asked questions

Why not only the first letter?

The last-letter level makes children listen to the whole word, which helps later with spelling.

What about words starting with silent or special letters?

Words whose first or last letter would be confusing (like œuf in French) are left out of this sheet.

How is Arabic handled?

Words starting with أ show أ as the answer; at the last-letter level, ة counts as its own letter.
