Counting to twenty and understanding what twenty means are different achievements, and children reach them months apart. Reciting the sequence comes first and easily; knowing that the last number said is how many there are — and that the digit 7 stands for that quantity — is the real work these sheets support.

🔢 Best for ages 3–6

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Count real things, touching each one. Counting aloud without objects builds the rhyme, not the idea.
  • Ask "how many?" after counting. A child who recounts from the start has not yet made the link.
  • Write the digit next to the group it names, every time. The symbol needs to attach to a quantity.
  • Stay inside 10 until it is solid. Ten understood is worth far more than twenty recited.

Frequently asked questions

When do children learn to write numbers?

Most recognise digits around 3 or 4 and write them between 4 and 6. Reversed digits are completely normal up to about seven and usually correct themselves.

My child counts to 20 but cannot say how many. Why?

Because reciting and counting are different skills. The sequence is a rhyme; understanding that the last word said is the total takes longer. Counting real objects and asking "how many?" is what closes the gap.

What age are these worksheets for?

About 3 to 6 — recognising digits, matching them to quantities and forming them correctly.
