Dots and boxes is a classic paper game that needs only a pencil and a friend. Players take turns joining two neighbouring dots; whoever draws the fourth side of a box writes their initial in it and plays again. It looks like luck at first, but children soon discover strategy: never draw the third side of a box, and sometimes give away one box to win many. The small grid suits a quick game with young children, the large one a long match for older kids and adults.

🔲 Best for ages 6–99

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Use two pencil colours so it is easy to see who drew what.
  • Only straight lines between neighbouring dots — no diagonals.
  • Count the boxes at the end with your child: it is counting practice too.
  • Ask after each game: “Which move made you win?”

Frequently asked questions

How many players?

Two is classic, but three or four can play on the large grid.

What does a player do after closing a box?

They write their initial inside and must play again straight away.

Which grid size should we start with?

5 × 5 dots for a first game (16 boxes), 8 × 8 for a longer one, 12 × 12 for a real match.
