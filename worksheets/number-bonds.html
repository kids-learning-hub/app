A number bond shows a whole and the two parts it splits into: 7 is 4 and 3, and also 5 and 2. The diagram is simple, but it does something written sums do not: it shows addition and subtraction as two views of the same fact. A child who sees that 4 and 3 make 7 also sees that 7 take away 3 leaves 4.

🔗 Best for ages 5–8

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Begin with real objects: put seven buttons in a bowl and split them between two plates in as many ways as you can find.
  • For each diagram, ask for both the addition and the subtraction it shows. "3 and 4 make 7" and "7 minus 4 is 3" from the same picture.
  • Spend extra time on bonds to 10. They are the foundation of mental addition and appear in almost every later strategy.
  • At the 100 level, numbers are multiples of ten on purpose: 70 is 40 and 30 because 7 is 4 and 3. Make that link out loud.

Frequently asked questions

What are number bonds?

Pairs of numbers that make a given number, such as 6 and 4 for 10. Shown as a part-part-whole diagram, they connect addition and subtraction.

Why are number bonds to 10 so important?

Because most mental strategies go through ten: 8 + 5 becomes 8 + 2 + 3. A child who knows the pairs to 10 instantly calculates much faster and with less effort.

What age are these sheets for?

Within 10 from about 5, within 20 around 6 or 7, and the tens version (to 100) around 7 or 8.
