ChinoKids — Learn Chinese by playing

ChinoKids teaches Chinese to children aged 4–12 the way children actually learn a language: short illustrated lessons, a lot of listening, and games that make repetition feel like play. 100 lessons, stories, songs and dialogues — all working offline, with no ads between exercises.

What's inside

📚

100 illustrated lessons

Vocabulary grouped by theme — colours, animals, family, food, school, body, time — each word illustrated and read aloud.

✍️

Guided tracing

Children trace Chinese letters and words with a finger, following animated strokes in the right order.

🎧

Listening & pronunciation

Every word is spoken. Listen-and-pick exercises train the ear before the child is asked to produce anything.

📖

Stories, songs & dialogues

Illustrated stories, sing-along songs and everyday dialogues put the vocabulary back into real sentences.

🎮

Eight ways to practise

Flashcards, memory pairs, matching, hangman, spelling, quiz, sentence builder and listen-and-pick — the same words, never the same drill.

📈

Review that targets mistakes

Missed words come back on a spaced schedule, and a parents section shows what has been learned — with badges and printable certificates for the child.

Frequently asked questions

What age is ChinoKids for?

It is designed for children aged 4 to 12. Pre-readers can use the picture and listening exercises on their own, because every word is spoken aloud; older children move on to spelling, sentence building and the dialogues.

Does ChinoKids work without internet?

Yes. All 100 lessons, the audio, the stories and the games are stored in the app, so it works on a plane, in the car or on a tablet with no SIM card.

Is ChinoKids really free?

The app is free to download and a large part of the content is free to use. An optional paid unlock removes the ads and opens the remaining lessons — nothing is charged automatically.

Is it safe for young children?

Yes. There is no chat, no user accounts and no personalised advertising. Anything that leaves the app — links, purchases, external pages — sits behind a parental gate that a young child cannot pass.
