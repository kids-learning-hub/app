Tracing single letters teaches shapes; tracing whole words teaches writing. The moment letters have to join, a child meets spacing, sizing and the rhythm of moving across a line — none of which appear when practising the alphabet in isolation. Each row here gives a model to follow, a pale copy to trace, then an empty line to write it alone.

📝 Best for ages 4–9

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Read the word aloud before writing it. Copying a shape the child cannot say builds handwriting, not literacy.
  • Trace the pale copy first, then cover it and use the empty line — writing unaided is where the learning happens.
  • Pick one theme and stay with it for a week. Repeated words become automatic; scattered words stay effortful.
  • The dashed middle line is the x-height guide: small letters touch it, tall letters pass it, tails hang below.

Frequently asked questions

Which languages can I print words in?

English, French, Spanish and Arabic. The word language is chosen separately from the site language, so you can read the page in Arabic and print English words — useful in bilingual homes.

Does it make a different sheet every time?

Yes. Words are drawn at random from the chosen theme, so pressing "New words" builds a fresh sheet. There are six themes — animals, colours, food, family, school and nature — with 288 words in total.

When should a child move from letters to words?

Once most single letters can be formed without a model, usually around 5 or 6. Words can start earlier if they are short and familiar — a name is often the first word a child writes willingly.
