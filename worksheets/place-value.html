In 47, the 4 is not four. It is forty — four groups of ten. This one idea, place value, is behind column addition, mental maths, rounding and almost everything children do with larger numbers. Drawing tens as rods and ones as small cubes lets children see the groups, so that the digits finally mean something.

🧱 Best for ages 5–8

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Before the sheet, bundle real objects: straws or lollipop sticks in rubber bands of ten. Children who have made the groups read the drawings easily.
  • Count the tens first, out loud in tens: "ten, twenty, thirty", then add the ones. Counting rods as "one, two, three" is the usual mistake.
  • Watch for numbers with zero ones, like 60 or 80. They test whether a child really understands that the 0 holds a place.
  • Move to the hundreds level only when two-digit numbers are quick; the same idea simply repeats one step higher.

Frequently asked questions

What is place value in simple words?

It is the idea that a digit is worth more or less depending on where it stands. In 352, the 3 means three hundreds, the 5 five tens and the 2 two ones.

When should children learn place value?

Tens and ones usually around 6 or 7, hundreds around 7 or 8, and thousands around 9. It is worth revisiting at every step, because every new written method depends on it.

How do I know my child understands it?

Ask "how many tens are in 47?" and "what is ten more than 47?". A child who answers 4 and 57 without counting has understood; one who counts on one by one needs more work with groups.
