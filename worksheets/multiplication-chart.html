Filling in a multiplication chart is more than copying answers: children discover the patterns that make the tables easier — the diagonal of square numbers, the symmetry that shows 3 × 7 = 7 × 3, the tens column. The 5×5 grid suits beginners, the half-filled 10×10 offers support, and the blank 10×10 is a real challenge to check whether all the tables are known.

✖️ Best for ages 7–11

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Fill in the easy rows first (1, 2, 5 and 10), then the others.
  • Ask your child to find the diagonal of square numbers (1, 4, 9, 16…).
  • Point out the symmetry: every fact appears twice in the chart.
  • Time the blank chart once a week and celebrate a faster finish.

Frequently asked questions

What is a blank multiplication chart for?

Filling it in practises every times table at once and reveals the patterns that make them easier to remember.

Which level should I choose?

5×5 for beginners, the half-filled 10×10 while learning, and the blank 10×10 to check that all tables are known.
