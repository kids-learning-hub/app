Rounding is the skill behind every good estimate: about how much will this cost, roughly how long will it take, does my answer make sense? It is not a rule to memorise but a question of distance — which ten, hundred or thousand is this number closer to? Children who see it that way rarely forget it.

🎯 Best for ages 7–11

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Draw a short number line between the two tens (40 and 50 for 47) and ask which end the number is closer to. Do this until the picture is in the child's head.
  • Only then introduce the rule: look at the next digit; 5 or more rounds up. Explain that 5 is exactly halfway, and we agree to go up.
  • Practise with real prices and distances: "this costs 38 dirhams, about how much is that?" Rounding is meant to be used.
  • For hundreds and thousands, check the child looks at the right digit. Underlining the digit being rounded prevents most mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the rule for rounding?

Look at the digit to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is 5 or more, round up; if it is 4 or less, round down. 47 rounds to 50, 43 rounds to 40.

Why does 5 round up?

A number ending in 5 is exactly halfway between two tens, so either choice would be fair. Rounding up is simply the convention most countries and schools agree on.

At what age do children learn rounding?

Rounding to the nearest 10 usually comes around 7 or 8, to the nearest 100 around 8 or 9, and to the nearest 1000 around 9 or 10.
