A crossword is spelling practice disguised as a puzzle. Because the clues here are pictures, children do not need to read a sentence first: they recognise the object, say its name and fit it into the grid. Where two words cross, a letter they are sure of helps them with the other word, so the grid checks their spelling as they go. Every click builds a brand-new crossword from 66 picture words, in the language of the page — Arabic grids even run from right to left.

🧩 Best for ages 6–11

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Name all the pictures together before starting.
  • Start with the longest word: it is the easiest to place.
  • Use crossing letters as clues for the harder words.
  • Write in pencil so mistakes are easy to fix.

Frequently asked questions

How are the crosswords made?

A program picks words from the 66-word picture bank and fits them together so every word crosses another. Each sheet number gives the same crossword again.

Does it work in Arabic?

Yes. Across words run from right to left, with one separate letter per square.

Is there an answer key?

Yes, the filled grid prints on a second page.
