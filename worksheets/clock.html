An analogue clock asks a child to read two hands that move at different speeds, on a dial that counts in fives but is labelled in ones. Almost every difficulty with telling time comes from that mismatch — which is why hours are usually easy and "twenty past" is not.

🕐 Best for ages 5–9

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Do the hour hand alone first, and stay there until it is easy. One hand is one problem.
  • Practise counting in fives before touching minutes — it is the same skill the dial demands.
  • Order the stages: o'clock, half past, quarter past and to, then five-minute steps.
  • Ask for the time on a real clock during the day. A wall clock at snack time teaches more than a page of drills.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a child tell the time?

Whole hours around 5 or 6, half and quarter hours around 6 or 7, and minute precision usually between 7 and 9. Digital time is often read earlier, but it does not replace understanding the dial.

Analogue or digital first?

Analogue. A dial shows time as a quantity you can see passing, which is what makes durations meaningful. Digital reading follows easily afterwards; the reverse is much harder.

Why does my child confuse the two hands?

Because they look similar and move at very different speeds. Cover the minute hand and work only on hours for a while — most of the confusion disappears once each hand is learned separately.
