Uppercase and lowercase letters

Capital to small, small to capital, or mixed. In Arabic, the sheet becomes the letter forms: beginning, middle and end.

Each Latin letter has two shapes, and some pairs look nothing alike: A and a, G and g, R and r. Children need to recognise both to read any real text. These sheets ask them to write the small letter for a capital, the capital for a small letter, or both mixed. Switch to Arabic and the sheet changes to what matters in Arabic writing: how each letter changes shape at the beginning, in the middle and at the end of a word.

🔠 Best for ages 5–8

How to get the most from these sheets

  • Start with the pairs that look alike (C c, O o, S s) to build confidence.
  • Spend extra time on pairs that look different: A a, B b, D d, G g, Q q, R r.
  • Watch out for b and d: say “b has its tummy in front”.
  • Read a short book together and hunt for capitals at the start of sentences.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the Arabic page show different exercises?

Arabic has no capital letters. Its equivalent challenge is the joined forms, so the Arabic sheet practises beginning, middle and end forms.

Does Spanish include Ñ?

Yes, Ñ ñ can appear on the Spanish sheets.

Is there an answer key?

Yes: each letter with its pair, or with its three forms in Arabic.
